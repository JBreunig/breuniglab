Understanding cell identity in development and disease

Neural stem cells generate the majority of cells in the brain. The Breunig Lab studies the molecular mechanisms that control how these cells differentiate during development—and how those same programs can be hijacked in brain cancer.

We combine developmental neurobiology, cancer biology, synthetic biology, genome engineering, organoid models, and single-cell and spatial genomics to understand and manipulate cell identity in the developing and diseased nervous system.

Research

01

Somatic & Genome Engineering

Developing precise, personalized in vivo and 3D model systems to interrogate gene function and disease mechanisms.

02

Brain Tumor Biology

Studying development, metabolism, cell state, and immunity in pediatric and adult brain tumors.

03

Neural Development

Defining molecular mechanisms that specify neurons, glia, and stable cell identities in the developing brain.

04

Organoids & Single-Cell Biology

Using human cerebral organoids, single-cell genomics, and spatial approaches to connect mechanism with phenotype.

Why we do it

The human brain requires the precise differentiation and integration of myriad cells to function appropriately. We aim to understand those developmental rules—and use them to build better models, technologies and therapeutic strategies for devastating brain diseases.
